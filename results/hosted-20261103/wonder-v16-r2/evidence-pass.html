Project answer

  1. Final-year group project, four of us, Sept 2025 to March 2026. From Ellie’s notes
  2. We built a self-service GPU job runner for the department's machine learning teaching cluster. From Ellie’s notes
  3. Students submitted training jobs through a small web form and the runner queued them on Kubernetes (k3s on six old lab workstations with GPUs). From Ellie’s notes
  4. Result: 140 students used it in the spring term and the average wait for a GPU went from about two days (the old sign-up sheet) to under 20 minutes. From Ellie’s notes
  5. Stack: Python and FastAPI for the API, Redis for the queue, Helm charts for deploys, Prometheus and Grafana for monitoring. From Ellie’s notes
  6. we used Kubernetes Jobs with a scheduler extender instead of Slurm, because the department wanted everything in containers and we needed per-course GPU quotas. From Ellie’s notes
  7. Problem: in week 9 jobs started dying at random, pods OOMKilled even with tiny models. From Ellie’s notes
  8. dmesg showed the OOM kills straight away and we wasted two days before looking. From Ellie’s notes
  9. Turned out the default /dev/shm in the pods was 64 MB, so the PyTorch data loaders crashed. From Ellie’s notes
  10. Fixed it by mounting a memory-backed emptyDir with a size limit per job. From Ellie’s notes
  11. Sam wrote the Helm patch. I reproduced the failure, tested his fix and wrote the incident notes. From Ellie’s correction
  12. Learned: check the node's kernel log first. From Ellie’s notes
